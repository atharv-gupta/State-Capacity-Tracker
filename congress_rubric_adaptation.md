# Congressional adaptation — read this before the rubric below

You are classifying a **congressional or federal-capacity item** — a committee action, a
hearing, a bill, an oversight letter, or a GAO/CBO product — not an enacted state-government
action. Apply the rubric that follows EXACTLY, with the adaptations in this section.

## The substitution

Wherever the rubric says "state government," read "**the federal government**." The four
competencies are unchanged in substance; only the level of government moves:

- `civil-service` — how the **federal** government hires, classifies, pays, evaluates,
  promotes, and separates its own employees, and where that authority sits. Schedule F and
  its successors, RIF and reorganization authority, OPM rule changes, Senior Executive
  Service reform, federal pay systems, probationary-period and removal procedure.
- `procedure` — the **federal** government's own procedural and compliance burden.
  Paperwork Reduction Act, OMB circulars, federal permitting and NEPA process, agency
  reporting mandates, grant-administration burden, the accumulated compliance apparatus.
- `digital` — how the **federal** government builds, buys, staffs, and oversees its **own**
  technology and data. Federal IT modernization, TMF, FedRAMP, agency AI use and governance,
  benefits-system failures, data governance inside agencies, the government as a smart buyer.
- `incentives` — the **federal** learning and feedback loop. Congressional oversight,
  GAO recommendations and their implementation, program evaluation, evidence-based
  policymaking, outcome-tied funding, authorization follow-up on existing law.

**Principles 1 and 2 in the rubric apply completely unchanged.** Capacity, not subject
matter: a bill regulating an industry is `none` even when it hits our keywords. Direction is
irrelevant: dismantling the merit system is a *strong* `civil-service` item, not a non-item.

State impact is **not** a criterion. An action that stays entirely inside the federal
government is fully in scope. Do not reach for a state angle, and do not down-score an item
for lacking one.

## Congress-specific rules

These override or extend the rubric where the two disagree.

1. **Elections administration counts here.** The rubric lists it under `none`; that
   exclusion applies to the state tracker, not to this one. Senate Rules and House
   Administration are the elections committees, and election administration is government
   operations. Election-system funding, voter-roll IT and data, EAC oversight, and
   election-security programs are `digital`. Certification, reporting, and audit mandates
   imposed on election officials are `procedure`. Pure ballot-access, redistricting, or
   campaign-finance policy remains `none` — that's electoral politics, not the machinery of
   running elections.

2. **Congressional oversight of an executive agency is `incentives`.** That *is* the federal
   learning loop. Hearings, investigations, subpoenas, document demands, and GAO reports
   examining whether a program works all count. When the oversight targets an agency's
   technology, data, or benefits systems, tag **both** `incentives` and `digital` — the same
   pairing the rubric already prescribes.
   *But:* oversight whose subject is a scandal, a personality, or a partisan dispute — with
   no examination of whether a program or system works — is `none`. The test is whether
   anyone is asking "is this working, and how would we know."

3. **Appropriations are `none` by default.** A funding level, an increase, a cut, or a CR is
   not a capacity event no matter how large. Appropriations count only when the action
   changes the funding **model**: multi-year or no-year authority, reprogramming and transfer
   flexibility, outcome-contingent funding, a new account structure, or working-capital and
   franchise-fund mechanics. A shutdown or CR counts only where it is specifically about how
   agencies operate through a lapse, not as a fiscal-politics story.

4. **Legislative-branch procedure is `procedure`.** House and Senate Rules Committee changes
   to how the chamber itself operates — floor procedure, germaneness, committee jurisdiction,
   amendment process, the legislative calendar — are the legislature changing its own
   machinery, which is exactly what `procedure` measures.

5. **GAO and CBO.** A GAO report on an agency's IT, workforce, procurement, or program
   performance is `incentives` plus whichever competency the subject matter hits. GAO's
   High-Risk List and priority-open-recommendations products are strong `incentives`. A
   routine CBO cost estimate is `none`; a CBO analysis of an agency's administrative capacity
   or implementation feasibility is `incentives`.

6. **Nominations** are `civil-service` only when the fight is about the position's authority,
   the appointment process, or the removal power. A nomination that is simply contested on
   the nominee's politics is `none`.

## Topic tags

The rubric's tag list was written for state government. Six tags are added for this tracker,
and they are usually the most accurate choice for congressional items — reach for them before
falling back on a state-flavoured tag:

`federal-workforce` (federal civil service and staffing), `elections-admin` (election systems,
voter rolls, election security, EAC), `appropriations` (funding, CRs, shutdowns),
`nominations`, `oversight-investigation` (hearings, letters, subpoenas, IG and GAO findings),
`shutdown-cr`.

Tags are descriptive and independent of competency, so a `none` item can still carry tags —
but only tags that are actually about the item. Do not attach a sector tag you cannot point
to in the text.

## Calibration

Most items are `none`, and that is the correct outcome. This matters more here than on the
state tracker: the input is press output from offices whose job is to produce it, plus every
bill referred to a committee, most of which are commemorative or narrow. Do not stretch. When
torn between a competency at fit-1 and `none`, choose `none`.

Judge the **underlying action**, not the framing. A press release headlined as a partisan
attack that in substance announces an oversight letter about a failing benefits system is
`incentives` + `digital`. A release headlined "modernizing government" that in substance
announces nothing is `none`.
